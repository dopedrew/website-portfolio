About Me

Defending What Matters

I enlisted at 19 and spent the first three years running helpdesk at Fort Lewis, the job nobody wants that teaches you more about how systems actually fail than any course does. From there to Camp Arifjan, Kuwait, standing up security for CENTCOM, then to Army Cyber Command hunting state-sponsored actors on DoD networks. The Army is where I learned that security is mostly discipline wearing a technical costume: the SOC, the checklist, the 3am alert, all of it rewards the same thing, showing up and doing the unglamorous part correctly, every time.

I carried that into civilian work: SOC analyst, then security engineer across defense and commercial environments, then founding C3Security while still holding down a full SOC rotation. I hold an active TS/SCI clearance with CI Polygraph and DHS Public Trust, and I am finishing a B.S. in Cybersecurity at WGU.

Outside work I build things that have nothing to do with a paycheck, almost always after hours, because the build itself is the point. See what I am building right now and who I am outside the SOC.

Beyond The Work

Outside the SOC

The parts of this that do not show up on a resume.

Nine years in, discipline first

I enlisted out of high school and the Army is still the throughline in how I work: show up, do the unglamorous part, do it again tomorrow. That is as true of a 5am lift as it is of a 3am SOC escalation.

Faith, read in order

I built Lectio, an offline iOS app that walks through the Bible chronologically over 365 days, because I wanted the events in the order they actually happened rather than the order they were compiled. In beta with friends now.

Training like the mission depends on it

Currently on a structured strength and size block, Helms-pyramid programming, readiness gated by actual recovery data from an Oura ring rather than how I feel that morning.

A homelab that runs my life

Roughly 200 automations on hardware in my house: a threat intel platform, a honeypot, a trading journal, a fleet of native apps, and a Tesla I monitor with a dashboard I built myself rather than the stock app.

Markets, modeled

I day-trade QQQ/SPY/SPX options with strict risk limits, and I build and run my own machine learning models, XGBoost, feature engineering, backtesting, for NBA and MLB, the same discipline of instrumenting a system and trusting the data over instinct that runs through everything else here.

Built, not bought

Every tool on this page exists because I needed it and did not want to pay a subscription for someone else's version of it. That is less a philosophy than a habit at this point.
