03 About

I enlisted at 22. My first three years were helpdesk at Fort Lewis, the job nobody wants. It teaches you more about how systems actually fail than any course does.

From there I went to Camp Arifjan, Kuwait, standing up security for CENTCOM. Then to Army Cyber Command, hunting state-sponsored actors on DoD networks.

The Army taught me that security is mostly discipline wearing a technical costume. The SOC, the checklist, the 3am alert: all of it rewards the same thing. Show up and do the unglamorous part correctly, every time.

I carried that into civilian work as a SOC analyst, then a security engineer across defense and commercial environments. I founded C3Security while still holding a full SOC rotation. I hold an active TS/SCI clearance with CI Polygraph and a DHS Public Trust, and I am finishing a B.S. in Cybersecurity at WGU.

04 Off the clock

What I build when nobody is paying me to.

Faith, read in order
I built Lectio, an offline iOS app that walks the Bible chronologically over 365 days. I wanted events in the order they happened, not the order they were compiled. It is in beta with friends now.
A homelab that runs my life
Roughly 200 automations run on hardware in my house: a threat intel platform, a honeypot, a trading journal, and a fleet of native apps. I monitor my Tesla with a dashboard I built instead of the stock one.
Markets, modeled
I day-trade QQQ, SPY, and SPX options under strict risk limits. I also build my own models for NBA and MLB using XGBoost, feature engineering, and backtesting. Same discipline as the rest: instrument the system, then trust the data over instinct.
Training on data, not vibes
I am on a structured strength block with Helms-pyramid programming. Readiness is gated by recovery data from an Oura ring, not by how I feel that morning.
